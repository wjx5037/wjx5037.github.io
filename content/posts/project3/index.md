## Project Goal

Design a hands-on museum exhibit for children aged 2 to 12 that makes basic circuits and physics tangible through a wire-loop challenge. The exhibit needed to be engaging, safe, durable, economical, and maintainable by museum staff.

{{< figure src="e10.png" title="Discovery Space Children's Museum, the client environment for Exciting Pass" >}}

## Interaction and System Architecture

Children guide a metal ring along a shaped metal path. Completing the path without contact represents success; contact closes the circuit and activates immediate buzzer and LED feedback. The functional decomposition informed the mechanical layout, electrical packaging, and safety interfaces.

{{< figure src="e2.png" title="Functional structure and system decomposition for Exciting Pass" >}}

## Requirements and Concept Selection

The team converted museum needs into measurable requirements: low-voltage operation, rounded edges, accessible service access, durable materials, visible feedback, and a compact table-height form factor. We used 6-3-5 ideation followed by AHP weighting and concept screening to select the final direction.

{{< figure src="e6.png" title="AHP concept-selection matrix used to choose Exciting Pass" >}}

## My Contribution

I contributed to requirement organization, concept generation and selection, SolidWorks CAD, and prototype refinement. My focus was translating stakeholder needs into practical design details: protected low-voltage electronics, rounded wooden surfaces, a serviceable enclosure, and durable 3D-printed supports for the track.

## CAD and Prototype Iteration

The CAD model fixed the positions of the track, buzzer, LEDs, and protective interfaces before fabrication. Prototype iterations then moved from a simple proof-of-concept to a wooden enclosure with hidden electronics, a hinged service door, and reinforced track supports.

## Design Value

Exciting Pass turns an abstract circuit into immediate physical feedback while accounting for the realities of a public exhibit: children, repeated use, staff maintenance, and safe low-voltage operation. The design process created a traceable path from museum requirements to a buildable prototype.

## Tech Stack

Stakeholder requirements analysis, 6-3-5 ideation, AHP decision matrices, SolidWorks CAD, rapid prototyping, 3D printing, low-voltage electronics, and design-for-maintenance.
